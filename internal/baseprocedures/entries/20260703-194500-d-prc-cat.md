Catch-up is the check-in move. Brief the user on where the project stands, threaded the way a colleague would tell it. End by asking what they want to move forward. Lanes below are injected fresh each run: the engine assembles the data, you compose the story.

## unit: compose

Produce a fast, useful catch-up for a competent colleague who knows the project but missed the week. **The lanes below are your entire input for composing the briefing.** Work directly from them. No other lookups, no follow-up reads until the briefing is delivered. Compose `briefing` in the exact format at the end of this unit.

**How to read the lanes.**

- Cluster by project thread (current relevance), not by kind or layer.
- The Focus block, plus aspirations and guiding directives from the session framing, inform threading judgment. Never restate them as content.
- Render focus as a single italic plain-language summary line at the top. Skip it when no focus is active.
- "Active and hot" carries pending work drawing attention. Its indented `→` sub-lines show references whose target is now closed or superseded: background, do not render them.
- "Open loops" is heat's inverse: the coldest active commitments, fresh and not acted on. Do not render it as its own section. Follow each entry's `→` upstream refs to its thread, weave it in there, marked as not yet acted on. A truly disconnected open loop gets its own short "a new direction opened" beat.
- Thread every graph entry once.
- A "Recheck" section, when present, lists clashes between entries and hand edits of committed entries waiting for a healing run. Give it its own short beat before the closing question, with the healing suggestion it carries.

**Tone.** Write like you give the catch-up at coffee. Find the interesting thing (the unblock, the surprise, the convergence) and lead with it. Thread events by causality: this finished, so this is now possible. Short sentences, plain words, room to breathe. Test each sentence: would you not say it to a person? Rewrite it.

- Swap the jargon. Say "mix up", not conflate. "include", not encompass. "start using", not operationalize. "use", not leverage. "find", "show" or "catch", not surface/uncover/expose (verbs). "show up", not manifest. "make unnecessary", not obviate. "important", not load-bearing. For hot/warm (heat scores), describe what draws attention.
- Passive surfacings ("X was uncovered"): use first person past ("we found X, caught it during Y").
- Abstract noun chains: describe the situation in plain words. For "breaks X by Y-ing Z", say the actual consequence.
- Internal references from entry text (slice numbers, AC numbers, version labels, fixture names, commit hashes): describe the work instead. The reader lacks that context. Entries use the banned words verbatim: paraphrase even when copying feels natural.
- One adjective is usually enough. Capture what matters, not every nuance. Real names of real things stay: `sdd view`, CQRS, engage, real entry IDs.

**Format: render `briefing` exactly like this:**

```
*Current focus: <plain-language summary, one sentence, or skip if none>*

**<Story-arc header for thread 1>**

<1–3 short sentences of trail. White space between paragraphs.>

1. <Action-tight item> (`<short-id>`).
2. <Action-tight item> (`<short-id>`).

**<Header for thread 2>**

<Short trail.>

3. <Item> (`<short-id>`).

---

**Other open:**

- A. <area>
- B. <area>

Say *drill A* or *survey* for the full picture.

**What do you want to move forward?**
```

- 2–4 threads, 6–8 numbered items total.
- Items and trail sentences ≤ ~15 words, no semicolons.
- Headers are story-arc phrases in bold, never kind names.
- IDs in backticks: inline in a trail when it names a specific entry, always at the end of numbered items.
- Drill items: 2–5 short labels, A–E.
- Do not print lane output verbatim. Do not number every entry. Do not re-cluster by kind or layer. No per-entry lifecycle commentary. No stats preamble.

## unit: junction

{{if eq .dialogueMode "autonomous"}}The briefing is your own orientation: no one is briefed. Decide what to move forward.{{else}}Present the briefing to the user exactly as composed. It is the deliverable, not raw material to summarize. Then the closing question is theirs: what do you want to move forward?{{end}}

Drilling is free while their answer is pending. `show`, `search` and `view` need no move, only the session handle. So "drill A" or "survey" is ordinary dialogue before the junction resolves. For a wide surface, dispatch the explore procedure to a disposable context instead of flooding this one. The lanes named only a slice of the graph, and their lines are pointers. Dialogue turns to a specific area? Widen and inspect before asserting what the graph holds.

{{if eq .dialogueMode "autonomous"}}Answer with a one-line reason: **pursue** carries `selectedThread`, your pick;{{else}}Relay the user's answer verbatim: **pursue** carries `selectedThread`, their pick in their words;{{end}} start the follow-up move (usually engage on the picked entry, or capture) as its own procedure with this instance as `parent`. **conclude** ends the check-in with nothing to move forward right now.
