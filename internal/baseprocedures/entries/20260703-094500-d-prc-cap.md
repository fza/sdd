The capture procedure is the shared spine for recording signals and decisions through dialogue: assemble a draft grounded in the graph, play it back for the user's explicit confirmation, judge the writing guide's findings on the approved content and fold them in, write through the pre-flight gate (the prose draft verifier over the grounding reading where `verify.mode` enables it), whose override is reachable only through the user, and verify the generated summary for fidelity. The guide runs once on the content the user approved and a later adjust gets a delta check of its changed lines; in an autonomous dialogue the guide and the verifier run before playback, since no dialogue edits follow. Supersede, close, and anchored captures are not separate moves; they are this procedure entered with start inputs (`supersedes`, `closes`, `anchor`, a pre-selected `kind`). The instruction units below are the authoritative capture guidance, served step by step against the running draft.

## unit: assemble

### lane: intro

Draft one entry — one idea, dialogue first. Report the fields below (batching them all in one report is fine); the step re-serves naming exactly what is missing until the draft is complete and mechanically valid.{{if ne .dialogueMode "auto"}}

A decision carries the user's own words for its reason in `reasonWords`, and a directive's for its intent in `intentWords`, quoted as the user said them. The reason states why, never what: words stating the decision itself never fill `reasonWords`. The user's words give no why? Ask openly, offering no reason of your own, not even as an option. The dialogue holds none of these words? Ask the user, end your turn, and draft after the answer. Never supply them. A wording change to the draft changes the body, never these quotes: report them again only with words the user gives for the reason or intent.{{end}}

### lane: lifecycleAndBody

**Lifecycle** (`closes`, `supersedes`). `closes` retires a target this work resolved; `supersedes` replaces an entry with this one. Either obliges the body, so settle it before writing: a retirement states why the target is retired, not merely that it is; a replacement carries the full current understanding on its own and says why each predecessor is replaced. A closure is often discovered while drafting — the retirement *is* the substance being recorded — so report it here when it surfaces, and report `null` to withdraw one you thought better of. Neither belongs in `refs`: an `addresses` ref reads as merely responding to the target, so the body claims a closure the entry does not carry and the target keeps deriving as open.

**Body** (`body`). The first sentence must work as a standalone summary — overview surfaces lead with it, so "Plan for X" tells a reader nothing; lead with what the entry is about. Fold the dialogue's substance into the description — trade-offs weighed, alternatives rejected, the why — so future readers get the reasoning, not just the conclusion.{{if ne .dialogueMode "auto"}} A decision's reason states what the user said in `reasonWords`; never supply one.{{end}} Every entry ID typed in the body must appear in `refs` (or be the supersede/close target), and every ref must be visible in the body's narrative. {{if eq .dialogueMode "auto"}}No user reviews the draft here: report the body inline as `body` and edit it with `bodyPatch`. A staged body file costs a staging call and a re-stage per edit, each one more request. {{end}}

**Layer** (`layer`): strategic — why, direction · conceptual — approach, shape · tactical — structure, trade-offs · operational — individual steps · process — how we work.

**Procedure drafts** (`canonical`, `class`, `procedureSpec`). A procedure additionally declares its identity and its workflow: `canonical` is the stable name everything binds to, `class` places how it enters (empty means move), and `procedureSpec` carries the workflow itself — `params`, `state`, and `steps` as the structured value this step's report schema describes, with the per-step `## unit:` guidance written into the body. Pull the spec reference fact `20260814-100000-s-prc-psr` before writing the workflow — it carries the field shapes, the gateable name vocabulary, and a worked example. The gate loads the declared workflow exactly as the engine will and names what fails.

**Refs** (`refs`, each `{id, kind, desc?}`, full entry IDs). The kind names why the pointer exists, chosen from what the body asserts — never a default, and the choices are trap-laden (`related` over-reached as a default, a terminal done "addressed"). The vocabulary lives in the ref-kind fact `20260828-160000-s-prc-rfk`: each kind's meaning, its direction, and when it applies over a sharper or weaker neighbor. Read that fact at least once before constructing any reference — it is the expected step, not an option. The optional `desc` says why this ref exists in this entry's narrative.

A ref may point across a repo boundary, written as `<repo-id>:<entry-id>`. Resolve-or-block applies equally there: a cross-repo ref must resolve to a declared dependency of this graph, or capture blocks — so confirm the target resolves (via `show`) before drafting, and a bare foreign ID is stored in full prefixed form. The lifecycle fields `closes` and `supersedes` never cross the boundary — a decision retires only entries in its own graph.
