# Making a thread findable: the annotation kind

Annotation adds no proposition of its own, only an index over propositions already recorded. It lays topic labels over entries already written, so a thread already running through the record becomes findable in one pull. A signal: noticed, not committed to. But its reader is most often a query, not a person. It exists to be filtered on, not read as an observation. Questions it answers are structural: which entries, under which labels, and, where a label covers only some, why those. The first cut (structure, not narrative) lives in the type-system introduction; everything here lies past it. Labels are the kind's only form today. The kind is broader: structure of other sorts can join later without changing what an annotation is.

**Membership is the whole effect.** Declaring a label over its references makes each referenced entry a member of that topic. To every later query, indistinguishable from the entry carrying the label at capture. Entry tagged itself or an annotation tagged it: one membership, not two meanings. Membership is binary, in or out, never weighted. So an annotation ranks nothing and says nothing about importance. Annotation is itself a member of every label it declares. So it carries no separate labels of its own: its declared labels are its topics.

**The moment has passed: that is the reason to reach for one.** Labeling at writing is the primary, zero-ceremony path: tag at capture when you can. Annotation is for when that moment is gone. The entry is immutable, the label did not exist yet, or one act should re-thread many entries at once. Timber framing: three entries landed over two months. A delivery arrived below the spec's grade; a customer reported a sagging purlin; the shop decided to change supplier. Nobody had a word for the thread when each was written. One annotation declaring `timber/stock-quality` over all three makes them one cluster, rewriting nothing. Rewriting is never the alternative. Superseding an entry just to change its labels replaces a record that is not wrong. Annotation adds the membership and leaves the original untouched.

**The references are the member set.** Annotation is about exactly the entries it references. By default every declared label covers all of them. Label belongs to only some? Annotation says so in its structure: that label names its own members from among the references. Body gives a line on why those were singled out. Same timber shop: "only the delivery and the supplier change also fall under `suppliers/sourcing`; the sagging purlin is a customer matter, not a sourcing one." One reference is a legitimate annotation. So is one act founding a whole family of labels over a dozen entries. Scale is not part of the kind's meaning.

**Labels are the payload, chosen as vocabulary.** Everywhere else a topic files the entry. On an annotation the labels are the substance; choosing them is the drafting. A label means the same cluster everywhere it appears. Graph stays coherent only if labels are reused, not reinvented. So before proposing one, look at what neighbouring entries already carry. Prefer hierarchical paths for families. Make each label specific enough to discriminate against future captures. Catch-all (miscellaneous, general, other) never supports a filter. Mint a new label when it founds a real cluster. Label with one member is a name, not a cluster.

**Minting is close to permanent.** No rename exists. Re-labeling runs through new annotations; old label survives the move. Labels carried at capture sit in immutable entries. Earlier annotation keeps declaring its label until separately retired. So a cluster's effective labels accumulate the stale beside the current. Getting a label right once is cheaper than evolving it later.

**Nothing is owed.** Annotation commits nothing, prioritizes nothing. Open, it demands nothing and puts nothing on the attention surface. Open indefinitely is its correct resting state. It never closes anything either: labeling a record is not resolving it. Clustering no longer wanted? Whatever entry carries the reasoning why retires the annotation. Vocabulary sharpens? Usual move is a new annotation beside the old, not a replacement.

**The body names the cluster, at most.** Referenced entries and declared labels are the annotation's whole content. Body's one job: cluster's name and a one-line gloss. No body at all is a correct shape, never a defect: prose density is no virtue in this kind. Body starts arguing something (why the pattern matters, what it calls for next)? That argument is its own entry, pointing at the same members.
