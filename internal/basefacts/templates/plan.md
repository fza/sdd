# Specifying an outcome: the plan kind

Plan records a decision on what must be true when a piece of work is done: an end-state, enumerated as acceptance criteria a closing act can be checked against. A decision: committed to, not noticed. It does not report that the world changed; it defines what change will count. Not every piece of work is owed one. Work needing decomposition (several requirements, design choices to hold together) earns a plan. Work specific enough to dispatch as it stands does not. Plan is never a tax paid before every act. Reader asks: what will be true when finished, how is completion recognized, what was deliberately left out? Good plan answers all three, shortest.

**An outcome, not a mechanism.** Substance is what is true about the world when the work is finished. Not the deliverables that will exist, their landing order, or their structure. Criteria naming only deliverables pass every mechanical check and still miss the target. "Are we done?" quietly becomes "do the pieces exist?", and all pieces can exist without the result they were for. "The information pack is printed and handed to every family" names a mechanism. "Every parent can say who to call when their child falls ill" names the outcome the pack exists for. Outcome cannot be stated without listing artifacts? Sign the outcome itself is not yet understood.

**Acceptance criteria are the residue of a design dialogue, not its replacement.** Plan's why is made in the dialogue that shapes it. Dialogue surfaces the outcome's dimensions: trade-offs, alternatives weighed and set aside. Criteria are what remains once it settles. Enumerating them first bakes unexamined choices into the definition of done. Plan's choices harden: ungrounded proposition, once captured, becomes structure later work builds on. So what the plan commits to traces to the record: decisions it reasons from, signals it acts on. Plan may close the deviation or question the dialogue set out from, at capture. That closure claims genuine resolution, not acquaintance.

**The depth of the record varies; the shape of the criteria does not.** Each plan settles how much specification it carries; the kind does not fix it. What the work needs weighs on it; a standing guideline may demand a floor. Neither settles it? Participants choose in dialogue:

- route truly open: criteria alone
- outcome cannot be judged without more: fuller record beside them (drawings, layouts, data shape, how parts meet, whatever this domain specifies with)

Dialogue produced trade-offs and rejected alternatives worth keeping? Attachment carries that design record; body keeps to the plan.

**State the end, then enumerate it.** Body opens with the end-state in plain words, whole enough to read alone. "Plan for the new blend" tells a reader nothing. Criteria break that stated end into checkable pieces, each a fragment of the same picture. Reading the list reassembles the opening statement; it introduces no commitments the statement never made. Delivery order, slicing, staging are routes to the outcome, not parts of it. They stay in the body, guiding work without being mistaken for what done means. State scope carve-outs explicitly: a line naming what the plan deliberately does not commit to. Later reader then tells a drawn boundary from a missed hole.

**One criterion, one thing to verify.** Each item carries one claim, confirmable or deniable alone. Usually one aspect of the outcome, not all of it. "Every loaf leaves the counter with its allergen list attached" verifies one facet of customers telling what is in what they buy. Item breaks when it welds two results into one, or states a quality too vague for any act to demonstrably meet. Verifiable means evidence can be given. Done signals that advance and close the plan carry that evidence: one done for the whole commitment, or several across partial deliveries. Test while drafting: what evidence would show this criterion met, and could a done carry it? Criterion nobody could evidence fails its purpose at writing. Evidence not obvious? Body says what completion is checked by.

**A proposal until the work proves it.** Open plan commits to an outcome; it is not evidence the outcome holds. Only completed work turns proposal into established truth. Closes when a done covers the whole commitment: every criterion confirmed with evidence, or its deviation named with reasoning. Criterion may be deliberately carved out at close, its why stated. Silent omission is the failure. Partial delivery advances without closing. Partial done points at the plan, says which criteria it covers and what remains, leaves closure to the finishing act. Plan whose work will never be built: retired by whatever entry carries the reasoning why. Stated rationale qualifies the retirement, not the kind carrying it. Closure ends the open status, not the plan's life in the graph. Judging what the work achieved comes after; closed plan is what it is judged against.

**Refined in place, superseded on a change of direction.** Active plan is never amended. Refinement is a directive pointing at it, sharpening or narrowing what it must satisfy; plan stays untouched. From landing on, the work must satisfy plan's criteria plus every open refinement. Delivering act answers and closes all together. Narrowing scope this way is the pattern working as intended, not scope smuggled. Supersession is for change refinement cannot express: direction shifts, several criteria need restructuring, or framing no longer holds. Then a successor plan covers the retired plan's remaining ground and names what changed. In doubt, refine first. Refinements stacked so high the plan's shape no longer reads cleanly: signal to supersede.
