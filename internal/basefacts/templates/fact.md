# Recording discovered knowledge: the fact kind

Fact records discovered knowledge: something learned about the world that informs later work without demanding any. Reference material a project looks up and cites. A signal: noticed, not committed to. Reader asks: what is claimed, why trust it, where does the fuller record live? Good fact answers all three, shortest.

**The claim carries how it was reached.** Write it so a later reader can re-derive it. Name the source it rests on: a document, a supplier's quote, what a customer said. Claim worked out rather than read off? Add the method, dated when the date matters. Verification is not the price of entry. Claim truly hard to check is still recorded, named as such, graded honestly in confidence. Knowledge the project holds is worth more in the record than out. Never acceptable: a claim with nothing behind it. State it in project vocabulary, exact words. Knowledge arrives through another entry? Borrowing that entry's words conveys the relationship, never imports its content.

**The claim in the body, the record at its source.** Figure is often the claim itself: "the drum roaster holds twelve kilograms per batch" is nothing without its number. So the test is work, not durability. Number stays out only when it is residue of the checking, binding every later reader to reconcile it. Past the claim, evidence scales its home:

- small observation: inline, dated
- record with bulk (measurement sweep, transcript): attachment; body keeps the verdict
- knowledge already in a durable document: point at it, never copy. "The kitchen follows the county's hygiene plan; the posted plan carries the steps."

Document stays the record. Fact says what it means for the project.

**Informs, never demands.** Fact sits off the attention surface: drives no work, nothing owes it a response. That is the line against the gap. "The group has twenty-two children registered for the autumn term" informs. Set against a licence allowing twenty, it demands. They stay separate entries even when one produced the other: fact holds the knowledge, gap carries the tension and points back at it. Same line runs inward. Entry prescribes (this must be done, by then, by whom)? A decision is smuggled in a signal's clothes. Possible consequences and remedies may stand as unsettled dialogue material. Committing to one may not. No automated check reads the prose for this: wrong-kind fact is caught at drafting or not at all.

**Cited and extracted.** Later decisions ground in a fact: take it as premise or cite it as empirical proof. So the claim must stand alone: it becomes the floor other entries reason from. Facts are extracted as often as written fresh. Claim about the world surfaces inside another entry (aside in a completed-work record, finding mid-dialogue), with own pull and no delivery of its own? It becomes its own fact. Host keeps the mention, not the full story.

**Answering a question.** Fact may close a question by answering it, carrying the question's context. Narrative restates the question's framing or names the unknown it resolves; reader need not reconstruct the connection. "Which provider should ship our parcels?" is not closed by "the courier ships for €4.50–5.80 per parcel" alone. Finding with no framing leaves the question open beside it. Judgment is on presence of that context, never on whether the answer is right.

**Corrected or retired, never fulfilled.** Fact has nothing intrinsic to resolve: no act completes it, later entries never "address" it. World moved or measurement improved? Corrected claim supersedes it. No longer true or relevant, no successor? Closed by whatever entry carries the news it stopped holding. Any kind except a question, an actor or an annotation, which state no findings. Closer qualifies by stating what changed: retirement merely restating the retired claim explains nothing. Closing where superseding was possible carries own rationale: "not superseding: no updated measurement is available, just retiring the stale value". Later reader then knows the missing successor was chosen, not overlooked.

**Enrolling in the pull index.** Some knowledge must be found before anyone knows to ask: how a cupping is run, how deliveries are logged. Material to pull, not guess. Fact may enroll in the pull index for that exposure, never on the drafter's say-so. Every enrolled fact costs a line at every session opening, so the call goes to the user. Craft is the title, written as a retrieval cue: enough to recognize that pulling helps. "how we run a cupping: sample sizes, scoring sheet, who calibrates" pulls; "cupping notes" does not. One primary topic groups it. Enrolled fact pays its length at every pull: read more often, terser it must be.

**Honesty in the small fields.** Confidence matches evidence: high needs strong evidence, not confident phrasing. Layer names the claim's scope, not its importance.
