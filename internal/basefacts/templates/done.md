# Recording completed work: the done kind

Done records that an act of work happened: meeting held, message sent, batch roasted and tasted, code shipped. A signal: something noticed, not committed to. It observes the world already changed, so the act lies in the past. Never intention, never work still underway. Reader asks: what happened, can I trust it, where do I look further? Good done answers all three, shortest.

**Say what happened, plainly.** Name the act in the work's own words: what was made, changed or settled. Concrete enough to recognize the delivered thing without opening anything else. No invented shorthand, no abstraction that summarizes the acts away. Stop where evidence takes over: never repeat detail the cited trace carries. Commit already lists changed files; attached protocol already holds readings. Body keeps to the act; detail lives with the trace.

**The doing is part of the record.** Noteworthy experience of the doing belongs in the done, told plainly: small deviations from the commitment, struggles, implications noticed mid-work. Not evaluation: judging whether work met its target is further work with its own record. Something noticed that stands on its own (problem, claim, open question) becomes its own entry pointing back here. Done keeps the mention, not the story.

**One act, one done.** Unit is the delivery event, not the concepts touched. Several changes checked and accepted in one landing: one done. Two acts: two dones, even from one session. Supplier meeting held and fix shipped same afternoon: two records. Anything that would stand as its own entry of another kind goes in that entry: choice made, problem noticed, claim about the world, open question. Done records the act, not everything it stirred up.

**A completion claim names what it completes.** Close what the act truly resolved: commitment fulfilled, gap fixed. Or a question the act's own record settles, when the answer travels with the done. Partial progress closes nothing: point at the commitment, say what is covered and what remains, leave closure to the finishing act. Graph holds nothing the act belongs to? Never invent a pointer for the form. Capture the missing holder first (activity or direction served, or gap answered), then record completion against it. Closure carries context: everything the closed commitment references is one step away. So never restate the target's references. Own pointers carry only what this record needs: an evidence source, or a dependency the act drew on that the commitment does not name.

**Evidence follows the act.** Cite the most specific durable trace:

- change to tracked material: exact revision carrying it (commit, numbered document version)
- result living elsewhere: where to find it (released version, published page)
- deliverable is a document (design note, tasting protocol): attach it
- nothing durable changed (verification, conversation): participant's own attestation in the body is the trace

Done that only retires a commitment delivered elsewhere carries no evidence of its own. It points at the record that has it; reproducing that record is the fault. Evidence is pointers, not measurements. Figure goes in the body only when it is the claim. Leftover counts and durations bind every later reader to reconcile them.

**What was done, not what was chosen.** Done describes execution. "Updated the label template so the misprint cannot recur": done. "Switched to pre-printed labels because in-house printing kept failing": decision wearing a done's clothes. Honest account needs a choice or justification? Decision is missing: capture it first, then the done. Deeper the choice, firmer the rule.

**Cover the whole commitment.** Closing a commitment with stated criteria: each criterion confirmed with evidence, or its deviation named with reasoning. Silent omission is the failure. Honest "this stays open" is not.

**The baseline goes without saying.** Claiming done already claims the project's standing checks passed. Verification required of every act is the claim's floor, not its content; restating it is noise. Name a check only when its outcome is part of the story: skipped, failed and accepted anyway, or tightened or added for this work.

**Status is terminal; the loop is not.** Done is a fact of execution. Nothing closes it; no status. Wrong record: correct with a superseding corrective done, never by editing. Work's story goes on. Judging the work (met its target? what did it surface?) is further work, its own done. What an act surfaces points back at the done that raised it. Later entries never "address" a done: no open concern to address. They build on it, reason from it, or are surfaced by it.

**Topics name the shape of the work.** Done's topic labels say what kind of work concluded, in project vocabulary (`roasting/first-batch`, `implementation/engine`). Subject needs no label: what the done closes and refs carries it.
