# Granting a part: the role kind

Role records the grant of a part: one named participant takes on a shape of contribution to this project. This actor participates as this. A decision: committed to, not noticed. A part is granted and relied on: from here on, project routes work and dialogue as if it holds. Reader asks: whose part, what do they hold here, when does something come to them? Good role answers all three, shortest.

**The actor comes first.** Role binds a participant the graph already knows: capture the actor, then the role. Role never introduces a person; it points at one. It binds to the actor's stable name, not to any single entry. Later refinement of the actor's record (corrected spelling, sharpened description) leaves every role standing exactly as written. Identity fix owes no successor roles. One conversation yields both halves (who someone is, what they do here)? Two entries drafted together, actor and role. Deferring the role half is a stated choice, never a silent drop of what was volunteered.

**The part is granted in dialogue, not assigned.** Role commits two parties at once. The project: to route work and dialogue this way from here on. The named person: to carry what the role says they hold. Its why comes from there: part is settled with the person, in dialogue, not decided about them. Child-care group: "Marta holds the settling-in period for new children" records Marta taking the part on in the team's dialogue. Not a rota drawn up in her absence. Entry records what was granted there. Part granted in the person's absence routes work to someone who never took it on.

**Shape enough to route by.** Substance is the participant's specialization and preferred contribution: authority held, decisions that are theirs, what is delegated away, what must come to them. Clear enough that another participant can tell when to bring work or dialogue to this person. Coffee roastery: "Ana holds sourcing and the roast profiles: she selects and approves every green lot, sets profile changes, and purchases over the standing limit go through her; day-to-day roasting runs without her" can be routed against. "Ana contributes to the roastery" is a role in form only. Naming what runs without the person carries as much shape as naming what waits on them. Shape belongs to one person. Role scopes exactly one named participant. A participant may hold several roles at once when the parts truly differ.

**The weight carried here, not the background.** Recorded confusion between role and actor is expertise, so state the boundary outright. What a participant brings from outside (training, years in a trade, a qualification) is actor material. The weight that background carries in this project is the role. Coffee roastery: "trained cupper, twenty years buying green coffee" describes the person; "every lot ships on her approval" describes the part. Drafting a role pulls toward biography? That sentence belongs in the actor entry. Role keeps only the authority the background grounds.

**Standing, not frozen.** Role records the stable part: what this person usually holds, not what they do in the current stretch. Standing is not permanent. Participation truly shifts (scope widens or narrows, responsibilities move to other hands, specialization grows into new territory)? Refined role supersedes the old one, as any decision retires when it no longer matches its recorded commitment.

**It reads whole on its own.** Prose introduces the bound name itself. Timber framing: "Jonas carries layout and joinery decisions on site; cut-list changes are his call" opens with Jonas. Record then makes sense read alone. Describe the part in the project's own words, not from a menu of standard titles. No fixed vocabulary of roles exists; inventing one adds classification without shape. Body speaks of the person and the part only. Rules on how entries link or validate belong to the machinery, not a role's prose.

**The person's exit retires the parts.** Role's ending is mostly derived: its distinguishing lifecycle property. Bound actor's identity closes (person leaves the project)? Every role bound to that name retires with it; no separate closing entries owed. Role can also be retired directly (part withdrawn, person stays) by any entry saying why the part stopped holding. Stated reasoning qualifies the closer, not its kind. Permitted but unusual: the cascade is a derivation, not a prohibition. Most endings are participation shifting (supersession) or the person going (actor's own closure already carries it).

**The layer is process.** Whatever depth the person's own work reaches, strategic direction included, role lands at process. It is about how this project works, who holds what, not the work itself. Topic labels rarely owed: named participant and part already carry the subject. Confidence may honestly sit below certain. Role is an evolving commitment about participation, not a settled observation; parts drift as projects do.
