The ranking judge that rates a draft's candidate entries gets two mechanisms, built in this order: three entries known to be far from the draft in every judge call, which set aside each candidate the judge rates no higher than them, and past entries replayed as whole checks, from which each graph learns its thresholds.

A check here is the judging of one draft's candidates in a gather.

## Problem

The judge's thresholds rest on recall stages over referenced labels, and background calibration seeds those labels from the graph's own references (20261119-105440-s-tac-5et). On this graph of 1,840 entries the chat judge of 20261118-162927-s-tac-pq5 holds its first recall stages little more than a minute after the server starts. That proof is easier than what a live draft needs.

- The seeded labels are positives only. A seeding call holds the references of one entry and no line that does not bear on it, the entries with the most references first. A live call holds up to 100 lines, most of which do not bear on the draft, and a chat judge scores a line against the others in its call.
- On one machine's ledger for this graph, the set-aside threshold at stage 0.98 is 0.200 over all labels and 0.078 over the live labels alone.
- Positives alone put the serve-in-full threshold at 0.012, below the set-aside threshold, so nothing was set aside. That defect, and a judge batch size that stayed at 1 after a failed call, are fixed on `fza-next` (d8ecfe30, cd78a009). Two weaknesses remain. A gather before the first stage, on a new machine or after a judge change, sets nothing aside. The thresholds rest on calls unlike a live one.

The drafting agent runs on a costly model and the judge on a cheap one, so each candidate the judge rightly sets aside is a line the costly model does not read.

How much a chat judge sets aside was unmeasured. The one earlier figure is for a reranker: in the replay behind 20261113-235104-d-tac-ypu it hid about 20, 30 and 43 percent of the listed entries at recall targets of 98, 95 and 90 percent.

## Decision

### 1. Known-far entries in every judge call

Every judge call of a gather carries three entries far from the draft. An entry is far when it is no candidate of the gather, shares no topic and no topic area with the draft, and lies more than two reference hops from every entry the draft references. The judge rates them like every other line. Their scores set the line of that call, and a candidate rated at or below the line is set aside.

The rule needs no label, so it holds on the first gather on a machine and for any judge model.

The line has two forms.

- Careful form: the lowest of the three far scores sets the line. The rule starts in this form.
- Median form: the median of the three sets the line. It applies once a proof of the rule for the graph is in.

What counts as a proof, and how a rule that hides entries is checked, is decided in its own entry. The change to the median form is built with that proof and is no criterion of this plan.

The rule never sets aside a candidate within two reference hops of an entry the draft references, nor one that two or more reads of the gather found. A gather has no such rule when fewer than three entries are far from the draft, or when the draft has neither a topic nor a reference.

`fza-next` holds the rule in its median form (8f34d10a, cf84d38d). The careful form and the change from it to the median form are not built.

### 2. Past entries replayed as whole checks

An existing entry plays the draft, and the entries that existed before it are its candidates. The entries it references are positives. The earlier neighbours it did not reference are negatives of low weight, because no label says such a neighbour did not bear. Sentences that name a referenced entry are removed from the replayed text. Replayed entries are spread over topics and dates and are not taken by reference count. The judge scores the replays in the background.

Each graph learns its set-aside and serve-in-full thresholds from its own replays, because the measured thresholds differ from graph to graph.

This plan fixes neither the weight of a weak negative nor the number of entries replayed per graph.

### Order

The far entries come first. They need no ledger and cover the first gather. The replay follows and gives both thresholds calls that look like a live one.

## Measured basis

The full tables, method and limits are in [the measurement on five graphs](./21-205340-d-tac-gyk/judge-start-measurement.md). The judge was `claude-haiku-5-5` through the `claude` CLI at low effort, with the production prompt and candidate line: 1,097 judge calls, 5.48 US dollars. The graphs are this repository's and those of four other software development projects, named project A to project D.

### Known-far entries

Ten past decisions per graph played the draft, and the entries each references are its needed entries. The large size is 764 candidates where the graph has them, else every earlier entry. The shares are for the median form.

| Graph | Entries | Set aside at about 65 candidates | Set aside at the large size | Needed entries set aside |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| This repository's graph | 1,840 | 46.5% | 62.9% | 0 of 39 |
| Project A | 5,050 | 37.9% | 60.6% | 0 of 36 |
| Project B | 857 | 40.0% | 56.6% | 0 of 33 |
| Project C | 437 | 39.6% | 56.1% | 0 of 37 |
| Project D | 250 | 42.6% | 46.6% | 0 of 34 |

- The median form set aside 0 of 179 needed entries at each size.
- The careful form set aside 24.3% to 39.6% of about 65 candidates and 37.8% to 54.3% at the large size, and 0 of 179 needed entries at each size.
- A line at the highest of the three set aside one needed entry on project A at the large size: the entry scored 0.10 in a call whose far entries scored 0.03, 0.03 and 0.25.
- The far lines shift the other scores no more than a repeat of the same call does. The mean shift is 0.022 to 0.047 and the mean difference between two identical calls 0.023 to 0.047. The difference of the two is at most 0.009, and its bootstrap interval includes or touches zero on every graph at both sizes.
- Between two runs, 12.8% to 31.5% of the candidates change sides of the line.
- At about 65 candidates, one to four of ten drafts per graph stay under 30% set aside, the lowest at 9%.
- The three smallest margins of a needed entry above its line at the large size are 0.04, 0.06 and 0.07.

### Replay

Forty past entries per graph ran as one judge call of up to 100 lines each: the referenced entries among the nearest earlier ones.

| Graph | Cut that keeps 95% of the references | Unreferenced neighbours below it | Unreferenced neighbours below the cut that keeps 98% | Serve-in-full line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| This repository's graph | 0.45 | 89.3% | 57.4% | 0.90 |
| Project A | 0.20 | 75.9% | 28.2% | 0.75 |
| Project B | 0.10 | 47.9% | 17.6% | 0.75 |
| Project C | 0.30 | 92.7% | 86.3% | 0.50 |
| Project D | 0.40 | 91.1% | 67.9% | 0.65 |

- The cut that keeps 95% of the references ranges from 0.10 to 0.45. This graph's seeded set-aside threshold of 0.20 keeps 94.2% of the references on project B.
- Project B's entries do not name their references in their text, and it gives the weakest numbers.
- With the naming sentences removed, on 9 or 10 entries per graph, the mean score of the referenced entries drops by 0.037 to 0.118. The mean score of the unreferenced ones moves by at most 0.009.
- The serve-in-full line is the lowest score at and above which half of the candidates are referenced. A check holds 3.2 to 5.6 candidates at or above it on average.

## Rejected alternatives

- Known-far entries alone. They give the serve-in-full threshold no basis. Estimated and not measured: on a gather of 764 candidates the 100 lines to judge stay full, and about 15% of the listed lines go.
- Replay alone. A gather before the first stage has no line.
- Neither. The gather before the first stage and the easy proof stay as they are.
- A fixed score cut. A cut at 0.05 set aside 47.7% to 73.1% of about 65 candidates and 61.8% to 81.2% at the large size, with no needed entry lost on the scores of the calls that carried far entries, and one lost on project D at the large size on the scores of the calls without them. A fixed score holds for one judge model's scale only.
- A line at the highest of the three far scores. It lost a needed entry, and one high far score moves it: 3 to 16 far scores per graph lie above 0.10 at the large size.
- Setting aside only what scores below all three far entries. Not measured. The far scores reach down to 0.01 on every graph, so little lies below them.
- A line of the draft itself as a known positive in each call. Not measured. It scores near the top, and no real candidate clears it.
- Judging every candidate twice and trusting agreement. Not measured. A miss the judge repeats stays invisible, and fewer candidates are set aside.
- Sharing reader labels through the repository. 20261114-105752-d-tac-tnk keeps the ledger on the machine, and the references already travel with the graph.
- A judge that quotes the draft and gives no score. A matching quote does not show that an entry bears on the draft.
- A test graph shipped in the binary. The meaning of a score depends on the graph.
- Judging clusters of entries. No measurement supports it.

## Accepted costs

- The far entries take three of the up to 100 lines of a judge call. They add no call.
- The replay spends background judge calls, 0.5 to 0.65 cents per replayed entry in the measurement.
- A candidate near the line lands on either side by chance between runs. The verdict stays with the capture, so a later gather of the same draft keeps it.
- The careful form sets aside less than the median form, 24.3% to 39.6% against 37.9% to 46.5% of about 65 candidates.
- The rule is off where fewer than three entries are far from the draft: on a small graph, and for a draft with neither topic nor reference.

## Not measured

- No run used candidate lists as production builds them. The lists are padded with random earlier entries, which are easier to set aside, so the shares are an upper reading.
- No label says whether an unreferenced neighbour was relevant.
- The calls with far entries ran once. The spread of the line between runs is known only from the scores of the other runs read against it.
- A recall cut on replayed texts without naming sentences. 29 to 37 referenced entries per graph are too few for one.
- A judge other than `claude-haiku-5-5`.
- The saving in what the drafting agent reads.

## Not in this plan

- How a rule that hides entries is checked and turned off, and what counts as its proof.
- The change from the careful form to the median form, which is built with that proof.
- The deep verifier's ranking calls, which carry no far entries.

## Structure

- Model: `model.FarFloor` picks the far entries and decides which candidates lie below the line of their call. Choosing the replayed entries, their candidates and their weighted labels from the graph and the ledger is model logic beside `model.RelevanceLedger.CalibrationBatches`, with no IO.
- Scoring: `relevance.ScoreWithFar` in `pkg/llm/relevance` puts the far entries into each judge call and returns the line of that call, in its careful or median form.
- Application: gather's scoring pass hands the far entries to the scorer and applies the line. `Application.AutoCalibrate` sends the replayed checks in the background and stores their scores.
- No new CLI command or MCP tool. Both mechanisms run inside the existing gather and background calibration.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A gather on a machine whose ledger holds no label sets aside the candidates the judge rates at or below the line of their call.
- [ ] The lowest of the three far scores sets the line of a call while the graph holds no proof of the rule.
- [ ] The rule sets aside no candidate within two reference hops of an entry the draft references and none that two or more reads found.
- [ ] The judge scores past entries in the background as whole checks against the entries that existed before them, with their references as positives and their unreferenced earlier neighbours as negatives of low weight.
- [ ] A replayed text holds no sentence that names an entry it references.
- [ ] The replayed entries are spread over the graph's topics and dates.
- [ ] A graph's set-aside and serve-in-full thresholds rest on its replayed checks.
- [ ] The configuration reference describes both mechanisms as built.
