sdd serves no hold-next line: the line of 20261114-121534-d-tac-qdb, the Opus and Fable gate of 20261114-190029-d-tac-a0z and its served guide are removed (b822411d), the every-tag anchor proof rule of 20261112-075604-d-tac-t4r is the only proof rule again, and every result lists the pieces served in full and not proved since, newest first and at most the 20 newest, as 20261113-121039-d-tac-ca2 set before hold-next replaced it (merged from `next/restore-unproved-list`). This closes 20261114-121534-d-tac-qdb as not achieved and replaces 20261114-190029-d-tac-a0z.

**Why.** Three deep-verifier captures with an Opus main agent on the gated build (fza-next 3ea6f35f) sent 13,989 held characters against 13,218 on three captures on the build before hold-next (d98b01fc): 5.8% more, where qdb's fourth criterion asked for at least 70% fewer. Result bytes rose 1.2% (519,696 against 513,289) and the gated runs sent one wrong proof against none. Opus sent proofs on every call under both rules, so, unlike the Codex and Sonnet main agents of 20261114-185019-s-tac-8aa that skipped most proofs under either rule, agent compliance was not the cause here: the hold-next lists grew to 5, 10, 18 and then the cap of 20 tags within one capture, because sdd names nearly every tag a later step may repeat, which made the named list no shorter than what Opus sent under the every-tag rule. The replay's 78% saving rested on exact foresight that the build does not have. The runs are recorded in the [Opus hold-next runs](./opus-hold-next-runs.md). The gap of 20261114-185019-s-tac-8aa stays open: every agent now gets the every-tag rule, and no mechanism is pending for main agents that skip its proofs.

**Rejected.** Narrowing the prediction and measuring again: more code and runs for an uncertain gain. Keeping the line with qdb open: a served surface kept up without a measured gain.

**Accepted cost.** The saving the replay promised is given up. The ruling is complete: nothing follows from it.
