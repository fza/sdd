A decision captured with a user present carries the user's own words for its reason, and for a directive its intent; the capture's assemble step requires them, and while the dialogue holds none it tells the agent to ask the user and end its turn, drafting after the answer. Autonomous sessions (`dialogue.mode: autonomous`) keep deciding for themselves and need no such words.

In the Codex capture evals behind 20261112-195218-s-tac-day, the user stated a decision without its reason, and no agent asked for it: each drafted a plausible reason of its own and played it back. The capture procedure already says to ask for a missing reason or intent and never supply one, but only as one sentence in the assemble step's body guidance and one in its intent guidance, while playback tells the agent to mark every reasoning sentence the user did not say as its inference. In the last batch the rule reached only 2 of the 6 runs, since an agent proving no tags never received that lane; neither of the two asked, and 5 of 6 marked an invented reason at playback instead, as the later, concrete instruction told them.

What changes:

- The assemble step of a decision capture outside autonomous mode collects the user's words stating the reason, quoted as the user said them, and for a directive the words stating its intent. Without them the step does not move on and serves the instruction to ask.
- Outside autonomous mode, playback's inference mark covers elaboration the agent adds, never the decision's reason or a directive's intent.
- Signals are unaffected.

Rejected: only rewording the rule and moving it to the start of the assemble step. The rule reached two agents in the evals and neither asked; two runs are few, but they give no reason to expect wording alone to hold.

Accepted cost: one more collected field on decision captures with a user present, and an agent can still put words in the user's mouth, since the engine cannot tell relayed words from written ones (20260924-232456-s-tac-f09); playback shows the quoted words to the user, who can correct them.
