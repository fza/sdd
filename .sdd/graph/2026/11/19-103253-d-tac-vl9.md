The grounding prefilter's calibration keeps labels across ranking judges, keeps scores per judge and per draft text, rests its thresholds on three recall stages, and reaches them through a background run of `sdd serve`.

## Labels and scores

- Labels are the choices people make in captures and the labels seeded from the graph's refs, closes and supersedes (20261118-162919-s-tac-2t0). A label names a pair of entries, not a judge, so every label carries over when the ranking judge changes.
- Scores belong to the judge that gave them, the one ranking judge of 20261118-162927-s-tac-pq5, and to the draft text they answered, keyed by a hash of that text. A new judge scores the labelled pairs again. A revised draft is scored again; no score of an earlier draft places its candidates.

## Recall stages

The thresholds rest on the highest stage the judge holds of the Wilson lower bound (95%) of weighted recall over the positive labels: 0.90, 0.95 or 0.98. A judge that misses no positive holds them from 35, 73 and 189 positive pairs it scored. Below 0.90 no threshold holds: no score sets an entry aside or serves it in full.

Until a stage holds, both readings use uncalibrated tiers by the judge's rank:

- `gather` keeps the top 100 the judge ranked to judge, lists the next 300 by title and counts the rest.
- The deep verifier's reading keeps the tiers of 20261118-173708-d-tac-euf.

The single 0.98 floor left a judge hiding nothing until about 189 positives were scored; 20261114-003118-s-tac-0vl recorded a bound of 0.9778 against it, so no reading saved anything. A lower stage rests on fewer labels and so on a less sure threshold. The 3 percent blind audit of 20261114-003118-s-tac-0vl and the grounding design's drift rule, which halves the set-aside threshold when more than 2 percent of the latest audit samples turn out referenced, act at every stage.

## Background calibration

`sdd serve` scores the labelled pairs the configured ranking judge has not scored, in the background: on first use, seeding the graph's own refs on a ledger without labels, and after a judge switch. It scores toward the next stage at once; once a stage holds, each further request waits until the server is idle, and the run stops when the top stage, 0.98, holds. It is on by default; `verify.auto_calibrate: false` turns it off and leaves calibration to `sdd grounding update`, which takes over from `sdd grounding seed --from-refs` and `sdd grounding rescore`.

A server quit stops every scoring call at once: it kills background `claude -p` processes and cancels any other provider's in-flight calls. Each request's scores are stored when it returns, so the next server resumes with the pairs still unscored and pays for none twice.
