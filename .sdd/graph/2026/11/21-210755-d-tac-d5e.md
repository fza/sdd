A rule that hides entries in a graph check may hide only after background replays of past entries have proved it, and its real misses are counted as facts; the blind audit samples go. The hiding rules are the two rules that set candidates aside by score in `gather`: the learned set-aside threshold (`model.Calibrate`) and the far floor (`model.FarFloor`), which sets aside a candidate the judge rates no higher than the median of three entries far from the draft, scored in the same call. One exception stands: the far floor in its careful form, the lowest of the three far scores as the line, hides from the first check without a proof. A needed entry is one the finished entry refs, closes or supersedes. A miss is a needed entry a rule hid.

## Blind samples go

Today 3 percent of the set-aside entries (`model.AuditShare`, picked by `model.AuditSample`) return unmarked among the main agent's lines to judge (`model.InsertBlind`). The main agent's reaction is the verdict: a referenced sample is a miss, every other sample counts for the rule. Above a weighted share of 2 percent referenced among the latest 200 samples, from 50 samples on, the threshold halves (`model.DriftStepDue`) and the far floor goes off for that judge for good (`model.JudgeFloorHealth`). The grounding design set the sample and the drift rule (D36 of 20261109-004506-d-cpt-udz), 20261113-235104-d-tac-ypu took them into the deep verifier's reading, and 20261119-103253-d-tac-vl9 kept them at every recall stage.

The mechanism has five weak points:

- Silence counts as proof. A sample the main agent leaves unreferenced counts for the rule, though an agent skims title lines.
- Few samples. This repository's ledger on one machine holds 42 audited entries, none referenced, under the 50 the rule needs.
- One event flips a rule. An ignored sample weighs 0.25 and a referenced one 1.0 (`model.WeightImplicit`, `model.WeightReferenced`), so one referenced sample among 50 is a weighted share of 7.5 percent against the bar of 2 percent.
- Random picks land far below the line. Scores pile up at the bottom: on project A 81.2 percent of a 764-line candidate list scored at or below 0.05. Most samples test entries no rule was in doubt about.
- The costly agent pays. The verdict takes the main agent's attention, on entries sdd believes do not bear on the draft.

Once the mechanisms below are built, the sample, its blind lines and the audit list of `model.PrefilterPayload` leave the main agent's graph check. The two rules that read the samples, the drift step and the floor's switch-off, give way to the steps below. The blind sample of the deep verifier's own reading (`AuditSample` in `internal/model/verifier_lanes.go`) is not decided here.

## Proof before hiding

A rule hides only while it holds a proof. sdd replays past entries in the background on the cheap judge, the ranking judge of 20261118-162927-s-tac-pq5. A replayed entry plays the draft: its finished text with every sentence removed that names an entry it references, against the graph as of its writing, which is the entries with earlier IDs. The replay builds the candidate list of a whole check, applies the rule and counts the needed entries the rule would have hidden. The proof is the Wilson lower bound (95 percent) of the share of needed entries not hidden, the bound the recall stages of 20261119-103253-d-tac-vl9 rest on. Without a proof the rule computes what it would hide and hides nothing. One exception stands: the far floor in its careful form hides from the first check, as the section on a machine that newly joins states.

The background run of 20261119-105440-s-tac-5et scores labelled pairs, one entry's pairs per call. It builds no candidate list and applies no rule, so it proves neither rule. The replays are the whole-check replays that 20261121-205340-d-tac-gyk plans for learning each graph's thresholds. The proof counts on those replays, run over the candidate list a whole check builds.

A proof names its judge model, the judge prompt's revision, the graph's size and the counts of needed and hidden entries. It expires when the judge model or the prompt changes, or when the graph has grown by a quarter since. A threshold whose proof expired computes and hides nothing until a fresh replay. A far floor whose proof expired falls back to its careful form.

## Steps in place of switches

Each rule holds one of three steps per judge. The far floor's two forms are those of 20261121-205340-d-tac-gyk: the careful form takes the lowest of its three far scores as the line, the median form their median.

- Full. The bound is at or above 0.98: 189 needed entries with none hidden, 280 with one, 361 with two. The far floor takes its median form.
- Narrowed. The bound is at or above 0.90: 35 needed entries with none hidden. The threshold sets aside only a candidate that scores below half its calibrated cut. The far floor takes its careful form, and it holds this step from the first check without a proof.
- Compute-only. The rule computes and hides nothing. The threshold holds this step without a proof or with an expired one. Either rule reaches it when misses take it down from narrowed.

A rule goes one step down on three misses from three captures, or when its bound falls below its step. It goes up only by a fresh replay. One miss moves no rule, and no rule is off for good.

## Real misses, counted as facts

Four detectors record a miss, each with the capture, the hidden entry, the rule that hid it and the detector:

- Rescue. The finished entry references an entry a rule hid in its own check.
- Verifier. A finding of the deep verifier rests on an entry the drafter's list hid.
- Person. The person names an entry the list hid.
- Scan after writing. In the background, the cheap judge compares the written entry in full text with the entries hidden just below the line. It asks a different question than the rule: does the hidden entry duplicate or contradict the new one. The same question on the same pair gives the same score, so it could not disagree with the rule. Estimated cost: about one judge call per written entry.

## A detection rate beside every count

Replays hide a needed entry on purpose and record whether the detectors notice. A count of zero misses then comes with the rate at which the detectors catch a planted miss. Without it, zero misses cannot be told from detectors that see nothing.

## The margin is an early warning only

The margin is the score of a referenced entry minus the cut. An entry that was shown has cleared the line by definition, so the margin says nothing about what lies below the line. A growing share of references close above the line warns that the line sits too high. It moves no rule.

On this repository's ledger on one machine, with the cut at 0.078: of 446 live references in 152 captures, 1 scored below the cut and 5 within 0.05 above it. Hiding by threshold began with the recall stages that shipped on 2026-11-19 (20261119-105440-s-tac-5et), so nearly all of these labels predate it.

## A machine that newly joins a graph

A machine that newly joins a graph starts at the team's level:

- The limit on entries in doubt, the cap on the lines a check hands the main agent to judge, holds with or without a proof. Today `model.PlacePrefiltered` caps them only while the judge holds no recall stage: 100 to judge, 300 more by title, the rest counted. Building the limit on every path is not part of this plan.
- The far floor runs from the first check in its careful form, the narrowed step, for every judge, as 20261121-205340-d-tac-gyk decides. It rests on the measurement shipped with sdd and not on a proof for this graph, so no changed judge, prompt or graph size ends it. Three misses from three captures take it down like any rule. The measurement: on five graphs, with a Haiku judge and the production prompt, neither the lowest nor the median of three far scores hid any of 179 needed entries. The lowest set aside 24.3 to 39.6 percent of about 65 candidates and 37.8 to 54.3 percent of the large lists. The median set aside 37.9 to 46.5 and 46.6 to 62.9 percent.
- One small proof record per judge travels in the repository: judge, prompt revision, graph size, counts. A machine with the same judge starts at the step the record proves, without a judge call.
- The machine confirms the record in the background by its own replays.

The proof record is not the reader labels that 20261114-105752-d-tac-tnk keeps out of the repository. That decision removed the committed relevance ledger: one record per pair of entries, which grows with every capture, adds a segment to every sdd auto-commit and was estimated at 7 to 9 MB at 100k entries. The proof record is one record per judge. It holds counts, names no pair, and changes only when a replay changes a proof. Labels and scores stay machine-local, so each machine still builds its own. What a team shares is the step a rule may hide at.

## Measured basis and costs

The far floor and whole-check replays were measured on 2026-11-21 on five graphs with a Haiku judge and the production prompt. The five graphs are this repository's graph (1,840 entries) and four software development projects, project A to project D (5,050, 857, 437 and 250 entries). The record of that measurement is the attachment of 20261121-205340-d-tac-gyk. The ledger figures in this entry come from one machine's ledger for this graph and cannot be checked from the repository.

- The build is the largest of the options weighed.
- A full proof takes 189 needed entries with none hidden. The ten drafts per graph held 179 needed entries, about 3.6 per draft, so a full proof takes about 53 replays whatever the graph's size.
- The judge calls of a replay follow the length of its candidate list, taken as at most 764 lines, the large size of the measurement. A full proof for both rules is about 160 judge calls, about 0.75 USD, on a graph of 250 entries, and about 425 calls, about 2.40 USD, on graphs of 1,800 and of 5,000 entries, per graph and judge.
- These are estimates from the five-graph measurement (1,097 judge calls, 5.48 USD), not from a run of a proof.
- A small file in the repository, and trust in a teammate's proof until the machine has confirmed it.

## What the proof does not show

- A replay proves on easy cases. A reference is an entry the author saw and named. Under a live rule a hidden entry seldom becomes a reference.
- The text keeps traces of its references. Removing the naming sentences lowers a referenced entry's score by 0.037 on project B and by 0.09 to 0.12 on the other four graphs.
- 12.8 to 31.5 percent of the candidates change sides of the far floor between two runs of the same judge, so a count of none hidden is one draw.
- The evidence for the far floor's first check comes from one judge. Other judges are not measured, and the careful form still starts for every judge.
- The blind spot that stays: a needed entry hidden far below the line that the main agent never opens, the verifier does not use, the person does not name and the scan does not reach.

## Gaps found in the code

- A rescued entry is recorded as a plain reference. `model.CaptureRelevance` writes the same referenced record for every answered entry, and the reading keeps no list of what the threshold set aside. The rescue detector needs the rule on the record.
- The judge's identity is protocol and model (`internal/llm/factory/prefilter.go`, `model.CanonicalJudge`). It carries no prompt revision, so a changed judge prompt keeps every score and stage. The proof's expiry needs the revision.
- Calibration on labels that hiding truncated moves the cut up. `setAsideCut` in `internal/model/prefilter.go` is a quantile of the positive labels' scores. Once a rule hides, positives below the cut stop arriving, and each recalibration cuts into the survivors. Derived from the code, not measured, and not solved by this plan.
- A verifier finding on an entry the drafter's far floor hid is unmarked. `model.VerifierRelevance` marks a finding's entry as a miss only when the entry was a blind sample or the verifier opened it from the entries its own reading hid, and that reading hides by threshold alone. The verifier detector needs the drafter's hidden set.

## Rejected

For the check of a hiding rule:

- Repaired blind samples. The verdict stays the costly agent's attention, and the events stay few.
- Replay proof alone. A rule that goes bad between two replays goes unnoticed.
- Hiding only what a shown line links to. A far entry has no shown neighbour, and the far floor already spares every candidate within two reference hops of a draft reference, so nothing could be hidden.
- Authors declaring an entry's reach at capture. A duty on every entry, and no check can verify the declaration.
- Comparing similar drafts, one that saw an entry and one that did not. Such pairs are too rare to carry a rule.
- Adaptive cuts per topic. Too little data per topic.
- Two detectors per miss. Misses are rare already.
- Re-judging the band below the cut and showing every entry whose verdict flips. With 12.8 to 31.5 percent changing sides, it would show much of what the rule hides.

For a machine that newly joins:

- Its own proof with no record in the repository. Every machine pays the replays and hides nothing until they end.
- Strict: nothing hides until the local proof, the far floor included. The first checks on a large graph run at full length.

## Structure

The split follows the planning contract 20260413-142536-d-cpt-ah1. The grounding code lives in `internal/model` and `pkg/application`, and the new parts go beside it.

- Model, no IO: the replay of one past entry, which takes its text without naming sentences, the graph as of then, the scores and the far line and gives the needed and the hidden entries per rule. Also the fold of replay records into a proof with its bound and expiry, the step a rule holds and its moves, and the miss record.
- Judge: the scan after writing is a second judge prompt beside the ranking prompt in `internal/llmops`. The judge's identity, built in `internal/llm/factory`, gains the prompt revision.
- Application, writes: `Application.AutoCalibrate` runs the replays, the planted misses and the scan in the background and appends their records through `RelevanceStore`. The capture's publish appends the rescue and verifier misses with its relevance batch.
- Application, reads: gather reads each rule's step and applies it. `Application.GroundingStatus` returns proofs, steps, miss counts and detection rates, and `internal/presenters` renders them.
- CLI: `sdd grounding status` formats that result. No new command and no new MCP tool.
- Not fixed here: the path and format of the committed proof record, the write that commits it, and how an entry the person names reaches the miss record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The threshold without a valid proof for its judge hides no entry, the far floor without one hides only in its careful form, and `sdd grounding status` shows what each would have hidden.
- [ ] A proof names judge model, prompt revision, graph size and the counts of needed and hidden entries, and it expires on a changed judge model, a changed prompt, or a graph grown by a quarter.
- [ ] A replay reads a past entry's text without the sentences that name its references, against the entries with earlier IDs only.
- [ ] Each rule holds one of the steps full, narrowed and compute-only per judge; it goes one step down on three misses from three captures or a fallen bound, and up only by a fresh replay.
- [ ] Each of the four detectors records a miss with capture, hidden entry, rule and detector.
- [ ] Replays plant misses, and `sdd grounding status` prints the detection rate beside each miss count.
- [ ] The margin of referenced entries above the cut is recorded and shown, and moves no rule.
- [ ] No blind sample appears among the main agent's lines to judge.
- [ ] The far floor takes its median form only while it holds the full step.
- [ ] A committed proof record lets a machine with the same judge start at the recorded step without a judge call, and the machine's own replays confirm or replace it.
