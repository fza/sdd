Main agents of Codex and of Claude Sonnet do not reliably send the anchor proofs of 20261114-121534-d-tac-qdb, under the hold-next line and under the rule before it that an agent proves every tag it still sees: most skip them, so they get repeated pieces in full again, and one Sonnet main agent that sent them sent 10 wrong ones. In the one Opus run the main agent sent them on every call, as the Claude verifier subagents did; Codex verifier subagents sent most of them. This replaces the earlier gap that named Codex alone, since Sonnet, sdd's default model, shows the same.

## Actual

Codex: in a live round of three deep-verifier captures with `gpt-5.3-codex`, one main agent sent an empty `held` list on all 18 calls after the first; the others proved 99 of 412 and 22 of 183 named tags. On the build before hold-next, three Codex main agents never sent `held`. Codex verifier subagents proved 19 of 19, 31 of 49 and 12 of 15. [Codex hold-next round](./14-185019-s-tac-8aa/codex-hold-next-round.md).

Claude, driven headless through `claude -p` with the same capture in every run: Sonnet main agents sent proofs unreliably under both builds. On the hold-next build one sent them only on its first `start_procedure`, one skipped them on 4 of 16 calls, and one sent 10 wrong proofs, 7 of them one tag repeated with a field name as its word; on the build before it one sent none, one almost none and one only its first 4 calls. The Opus main agent sent them on every call after the first, none wrong. Verifier subagents, Sonnet included, sent them on every call. [Claude hold-next runs](./14-185019-s-tac-8aa/claude-hold-next-runs.md).

## What the runs settle

The fourth criterion of 20261114-121534-d-tac-qdb is not met for Sonnet: its 80% fewer proof characters come from agents that prove little, and wrong proofs rose to 12 against 8 on the build before it (1 in its two complete runs), while result bytes were about 7% higher per run, within run-to-run variation. The premise of 20261114-181553-d-tac-xvr, that Claude agents send proofs on most calls, holds for Opus, not for Sonnet. For Codex the criterion is met only on its letter: 92% fewer proof characters and 5.4% fewer result bytes, with 1 wrong proof against 0, from main agents that prove little; the playback step's instruction text (about 2.9 KB) and the framing block beside it (about 2.6 KB) came back in full at every playback.

## Expected

A main agent sends the proofs the served instructions name, so repeated pieces serve as pointers, whatever its model.

## Not settled

Why main agents skip `held` while subagents of the same model send it: the length of the main dialogue, the served wording, the tool schema, or the models' tool-call habits. The branch `pr/hold-next-codex-rethink` keeps the ungated hold-next state.
