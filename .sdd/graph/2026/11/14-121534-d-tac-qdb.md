sdd names which anchor proofs an agent sends: each result ends with a hold-next line of at most 20 tags whose pieces the next serve may repeat. This replaces the rule of the anchor proof (20261112-075604-d-tac-t4r) that an agent lists every tag it still sees on every call; the proof itself, a tag and the word after it, stays. Its rule that a missing proof gets the full text at once stays too, for entries of any length. It replaces the reminder of 20261113-121039-d-tac-ca2 as well, which lists the 20 newest unproved pieces: the replay below shows that the pieces served again sit far behind the newest ones, so a newest-first list misses them, while a list of what the next serve can repeat names them.

**Why.** Replayed over 58 agent contexts and 2,430 calls from seven projects, the held lists cost 1.18 M characters of agent output, with a median of 19 and up to 236 tags per call, and they grow with every distinct piece a context receives. Pieces served again are mostly old ones (the type-system overview, a step's instruction lanes, the report schema): in a long capture their tags sit 100 to 200 places back. A cap on the newest tags therefore loses: newest 20 raises total traffic to 4.99 M characters, newest 40 to 2.16 M. A list sdd names from what its next serve can repeat brings the total to 0.26 M characters with 30 of 4,548 pointer serves missed under exact foresight; a real prediction misses more. Live runs of one capture to its guide-review step (12 calls) are too short to separate the policies: newest 10 added about 8 KB per capture and newest 20 nothing, both within a run-to-run noise of about 17 KB. The measurements are attached: [held-list measurements](./14-121534-d-tac-qdb/held-measurements.md).

**Hold-next line.** sdd orders the named tags by how likely the next serve repeats each piece: the current step's instruction lanes and report schema first, then the type-system overview and kind guide while a capture runs, then entries the open reading depends on. The agent sends exactly the named tags with their words. The line writes tag ids without the bracket form, as 20261113-121039-d-tac-ca2 settled, so no word follows a tag there; gather brief lines stay off it, since they always serve in full. A piece sdd serves in full that it could have pointed to is added to the hold-next lines for the rest of its move, so the set grows only where a session repeats pieces. K, the cap on the hold-next line, is not fixed: it is the number of pieces the next step can repeat, at most 20.

**No tag challenge.** sdd does not ask the agent for a long entry's tag before serving it again. Such a challenge costs one extra request, and that request rereads the agent's whole cached context, a median of about 255,000 tokens at an sdd call. Measured over the same transcripts, the challenge is cheaper than serving the entry in full again at 11% of calls for a 2,000-character entry, and at half of them only from about 7,100 characters, a size about 10% of graph files reach (their 90th percentile is 7,014 characters). Section 3 of the attached measurements holds the numbers.

**Rejected.** A cap on the newest N tags, with N fixed or adapted per session: the replay needs N near 200 for a long capture, which removes the saving. The agent ranking tags by how often pieces came back: it cannot know what the next step serves and spends output on the bookkeeping.

**Accepted costs.** One more line per result (about 26 characters per call in the replay); a piece sdd fails to predict is served in full once; the served instructions change what agents send.

## Fulfilled when

- Every serve ends with a hold-next line of at most 20 tags, ordered as above, and the served instructions tell the agent to send exactly those.
- A piece served in full that could have been a pointer joins the following hold-next lines of its move.
- An unproved entry is served in full again, whatever its length.
- A capture with a deep verifier sends at least 70% fewer held characters than with every tag, with total result bytes no higher and no more wrong proofs, over 3 runs. This replaces the criterion of 20261113-121039-d-tac-ca2 that repeats fall under 15 percent of result characters.
