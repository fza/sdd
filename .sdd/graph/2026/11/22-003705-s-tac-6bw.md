title: Equal call and pass timeouts block halving
summary: A timed-out judge call never halves its batch in graph checks or verifier ranking, because one call's time limit equals the 45-second pass deadline. The probe of `relevance.Halving` over `Bounded` confirms halving only when the call limit is shorter than the pass deadline. The docs and the shared judge signal claim timeouts halve; the fix direction, likely a lower default call limit as commit 804f181e had, is unsettled.

With default settings a judge call that times out is never halved in a graph check or in the deep verifier's ranking pass, because the time limit of one call equals the deadline of the pass it runs in, 45 seconds each. `relevance.Halving` halves a timed-out call only while the pass still lives, so in both places only a broken or incomplete answer halves a batch and lowers the remembered size. This is derived from the code on `fza-next` at 3f694cab and from a probe of the exported scorer API. No run of a graph check or a verifier pass showed it.

## Expected

The configuration reference states the halving without that condition. `website/src/content/docs/reference/configuration.md`, in the row of `llm_prefilter.protocol`, reads "A call whose answer breaks, lacks a score or runs past `llm_prefilter.timeout` is sent again in halves, down to one entry a call beside the far ones". The delivery of the chat judge, 20261118-162927-s-tac-pq5, says the same: "A broken or incomplete answer, or a timeout, halves the batch". For the verifier's pass the reference names the broken answer alone, in its section on ranking the deep reading: "a call whose answer breaks is sent again in halves within it".

## Where

Three time limits share the default of 45 seconds.

- The limit of one call: `defaultPrefilterTimeout` in `internal/llm/factory/prefilter.go` (line 20). `llm_prefilter.timeout` replaces it when set (lines 37 to 46), and `relevance.Bounded` applies it to every call (line 92). The comment on the constant reads "a call is bounded by the pass deadline".
- The deadline of a graph check's scoring pass: `prefilterDeadline` in `pkg/application/prefilter.go` (line 22), a constant without a setting, passed to `scoreCandidates` (line 79).
- The deadline of the verifier's ranking pass: `DefaultRankingTimeout` in `internal/model/verifier.go` (line 129), the default of `verify.ranking_timeout`, passed to `scoreCandidates` in `pkg/application/ranking.go` (line 92).

Commit 804f181e set `defaultPrefilterTimeout` to 20 seconds. Its comment on the constant read "a call is bounded well below gather's 45s pass deadline, so a slow call leaves time to send its halves". Commit 6f760d64, which took the graph-check cuts off `fza-next`, set the limit back to 45 seconds and the comment back to the one quoted above.

`scoreCandidates` puts every call of a pass under one context with that deadline (`pkg/application/prefilter.go`, line 163). `relevance.Bounded` derives the context of a call from it (`pkg/llm/relevance/decorate.go`, lines 28 to 34). A call starts at or after the start of its pass, so its own deadline never lies before the pass deadline, and its context ends when the pass context ends.

`Halving` returns the error without halving once the context it was called with has ended (`pkg/llm/relevance/halving.go`, line 171: `if n == 1 || ctx.Err() != nil || !halvable(err)`). In both passes that is the pass context. The doc comment of `Halving` states the condition: a batch that "runs out the inner call's deadline while ctx lives is scored again in halves". The test `TestHalving_HalvesABatchThatTimesOut` in `pkg/llm/relevance/halving_test.go` holds both branches: a call past its own deadline halves, and the caller's deadline ends the request and remembers no size.

## How it shows

A probe program ran `Halving` over `Bounded` against a scorer that answers at most 10 candidates and holds a larger call until its context ends, with a request of 40 candidates. The limits are scaled down from the shipped ones.

| Limit of one call | Deadline of the pass | Inner calls, by candidates | Scores returned | Size remembered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 ms | 300 ms | 40 | 0 | none |
| 100 ms | 2 s | 40, 20, 10, 10, 10, 10 | 40 | 10 |
| 300 ms | 400 ms | 40, 20 | 0 | 20 |

- With equal limits, the first row, the one call returns `context deadline exceeded` with the pass over. `Halving` sends no half and does not call `Lower`, so the next pass starts at the same size.
- The timeout halves a batch only where the limit of one call is shorter than the pass. That holds with `llm_prefilter.timeout` set below 45 seconds, in the verifier's pass with `verify.ranking_timeout` set above `llm_prefilter.timeout`, and in the scoring of `sdd grounding update` and of the background calibration (20261119-105440-s-tac-5et). That scoring sets no pass deadline of its own: `scoreSubject` in `pkg/application/grounding_scoring.go` calls the scorer under the caller's context.
- A set `llm_prefilter.timeout` makes the `llm_prefilter` block non-empty. While the block is set, the Claude host's default judge, Haiku 5.5 through the `claude` CLI, does not apply (`ClaudeHostRanking` in `internal/model/grounding.go`), and the judge becomes `chat` on the `llm` provider and model. A shorter call limit for that default judge therefore needs a whole `llm_prefilter` block that names its provider and model as well.
- The comment on `DefaultRankingTimeout` says of the value that "a chat judge's slowest measured call and one retry of it fit". A call that runs to its limit of 45 seconds leaves no time for a second call.

Recorded durations of the chat judge:

- A call of 100 candidates took 11.7 s at p50 and 16.6 s at p90 (20261118-162927-s-tac-pq5).
- On one machine with a stored batch size of 1, one call took 22 s at the median and 41 s at the 90th percentile. The measurement attached to 20261121-223938-d-tac-rqi records it.
- On that machine a graph check of 377 calls of one candidate each ended with all 377 in `context deadline exceeded` at the pass deadline (20261121-232710-s-tac-khe). A call of one candidate cannot be halved, so that check does not show this defect. It shows that calls of this judge run into the pass deadline. The batch size fixes of that entry repair how a halved size grows back and leave the timeout branch as it was.

## Cost

- A chat judge whose batch does not answer within 45 seconds scores nothing of that batch in any graph check and any verifier ranking pass, and the size stays. Each such pass waits its full deadline and leaves every candidate of the batch unscored.
- The size falls only through a broken answer, or in `sdd grounding update` and the background calibration. The background calibration stops once the judge holds the 0.98 recall stage.

## The planned time limit of a check

The plan 20261121-221907-d-tac-4rg makes the time limit of a graph check a setting: 1 minute by default, 0 for no limit, and at least 30 seconds otherwise. With 1 minute and a call limit of 45 seconds one halving fits, and the halves have 15 seconds, as in the third row of the probe. With 30 seconds no call reaches its own limit. The plan does not state how the two limits relate, and it lists `verify.ranking_timeout` as not covered.

## Not known

- How often a batch of more than one candidate takes 45 seconds. Of the recorded durations above, only those of the call of 100 candidates are of such a batch.
- Whether this took part in any observed failure. A stored size of 1 explains the recorded check of 377 calls.
- Whether the wait for a free slot counts against a call's 45 seconds in practice. By the composition it does: `relevance.Limited` holds 8 chat calls in flight (`defaultChatConcurrency`) inside `Bounded`, and a graph check starts up to 16 calls at once (`prefilterInFlight`).
- Whether 6f760d64 set the call limit back to 45 seconds on purpose. Its message names the graph-check cuts only.
- Which fix is wanted. Three directions are open, none settled: a default call limit below the pass deadline, as 804f181e had it, a pass deadline that follows the call limit, or a corrected reference text.
