A capture's publish no longer fails once an earlier verifier child has ended, and a retry after a failure behind the write no longer publishes a second entry.

Two defects, reported from another project's graph on fza.51 and fza.52, wrote a duplicate entry there. First, every new deep exchange ends the previous child of the verify procedure (20261109-010000-d-prc-vfy) by design, yet publish read the verifier's openings by replaying every verify fork, ended ones included, and an ended child is refused. Any capture with two or more deep exchanges, where publish had openings of the verifier to record, failed after its entry was committed. Second, the retry re-ran the whole write: the late-entries check ran before the publication-key lookup and counted the capture's own entry as late, so the draft got a new ID and a duplicate was published, with `entry.written` fired without an entry. Both broke the event-sourced session model's rule that a retry recognizes effects already applied (20260914-113737-d-cpt-qst), and the second bypassed the publication key that capture publication introduced for exactly that (20260915-085829-s-tac-awi), which 20260915-102225-d-cpt-7pk names among the few preconditions the write itself guards.

The fix has three parts:

- Each verifier delivery carries the entries the delegate read in full. The capture's fork record keeps them, logged on the delivery's received event, and publish reads that record instead of replaying children.
- The write looks up its own publication before the late-entries check, and `entry.written` fires only with an entry ID.
- The engine records a committed external effect at once (`Context.Commit`, a fork-only `mutation_committed` event), so a retry, live or after a resume, continues after the commit. The recheck and relevance steps behind the write commit their own markers and are skipped on retry.

Rejected: only reordering the lookup, because every other step that commits and can fail afterwards keeps the risk; only the engine record, because the wrong check order still bypasses the publication guard; logging the failures behind the write instead of failing, because sdd fails loud.

Accepted costs: sessions logged before the fix carry no fork reads, so their verifier openings are lost, though their publish no longer fails; fza.52 and older builds refuse a session log holding the new event.

Settled: built as `d7504745`, `b60f0fa2`, `5924b8b0`, `1720fdbc` and `34361d9a` on `next/fix-capture-publish`, merged into `fza-next`. Each defect was reproduced by a test that failed first, and the full isolated suite, vet and lint pass.
