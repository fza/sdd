People working with the graph have no durable, named clusters to reason over in place of the whole web of entries: topic labels give entries a shared membership but carry no meaning of their own, and the clusters a session names are lost when it ends.

**Expected.** Stated by Christopher in this dialogue: the graph holds a complex picture built from many pieces, and people need clusters with names to work with it instead of a web of entries; a concept or term can be one such cluster. Such clusters are durable rather than created anew each session, form sets over entries that may nest and overlap, carry a definition and content of their own, and commit nothing, so they never compete with decisions or other entries. They arise both explicitly, named in dialogue, and emergently, found in the graph. A second reason, also Christopher's, concerns the experience of working with the graph rather than its technical size: as a project grows and ages, nobody can take in the whole graph, so working with it needs divide and conquer, looking at a subgraph to reason over instead of the whole.

**Actual.** From reading the graph and from this session's work:

- Topics already give overlapping sets, nested along one path tree, which answered the request for emergent clustering in 20260410-110420-s-cpt-s43. A label is only a string, though: no entry owns or defines it, and labels cannot be renamed or merged (20260601-162249-s-cpt-k1b).
- The annotation kind lays labels over existing entries and is content-less by design: its body names the cluster in one line at most, while the kind leaves room for other sorts of structure (20260818-110500-s-prc-ann). None has been captured since June, and the engine has no capture path for one (20260722-141909-s-tac-6rd).
- A definition tied to a topic exists only for facts enrolled in the pull index, through their title and topic (20260719-133529-d-cpt-qhn). SDD's own terms are to ship as one vocabulary fact (20260904-141736-d-cpt-xob); a project's own domain terms have no home (20260818-143339-s-cpt-8h2).
- Named clusters live outside entries or not at all: catch-up derived its clusters on each run (20260505-153654-d-tac-qom), the vision landscape's regions exist only in a plan's attachment (20260818-133112-d-tac-okx), and this session's overview of open concepts computed clusters over references and embeddings and named them itself, then had to rename most of them because the names matched nothing the graph held.

**Deviation.** Every session that needs to see the graph in clusters re-derives them under names nobody defined, and a person who wants to reason over one part of the graph has no durable, defined subgraph to open, while the mechanisms that could hold them stop at membership, in topics and annotations, or at single indexed facts.

**Unsettled candidates.** Discussed with Christopher and deliberately left for a future session; the attached record carries the reasoning.

- A cluster entry holds the meaning and its topic label is the cluster's canonical handle, with membership staying in topics. A canonical with aliases, as an actor carries them (20260818-110000-s-prc-act), would let a superseding cluster entry keep old labels resolving, which would answer 20260601-162249-s-cpt-k1b.
- Cluster entries hold meaning and annotations hold structure, both membership and the placement of clusters inside other clusters, so structure can change without superseding meaning.
- A reference to a cluster entry stays distinct from membership in its topic: the reference states what an entry's reasoning relies on and adds to the cluster's heat, the graph's measure of how much recent entries reference it, while membership states what the entry is about.
- Nesting through relations between clusters, with a parent's members derived on read as the union of its parts, in line with 20260604-152617-s-cpt-dnh, because a label path places each cluster under exactly one parent and cannot express a cluster that belongs to two larger ones.
- Emergence through a procedure that runs clustering as an engine capability, compares it against named clusters and proposes unnamed ones, likely missing members, drifted clusters and undefined labels; only dialogue names a cluster.
- Whether the domain vocabulary of 20260818-143339-s-cpt-8h2 is answered by named clusters, with a domain term as one cluster, or needs more of its own.
- The carrier: a fact with a declared class as 20260904-141736-d-cpt-xob uses, an annotation allowed a body, or a new signal kind.
