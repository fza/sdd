Merging a duplicated actor by superseding both duplicates with one new actor entry leaves two identity chains instead of one. Observed in another project on sdd 0.18.0-alpha.2 through engine capture. The same person had been captured twice, as two separate actor chains with the same canonical. A new actor entry superseded the head of each chain to unify them into one identity:

- Pre-flight blocked the write with `actor-canonical-reused`, naming one of the superseded heads as the conflicting chain. The user overrode it.
- After the write, status derivation reports both predecessors superseded by the new entry, yet the graph health check still reports the canonical in two actor-identity chains and flags the new head and its first predecessor.
- The role bound to the canonical derives `cascade-orphan` and drops out of the participants section.

The expected outcome of the merge is one chain under the new head, no health warning, and the role still active. The actor fact 20260818-110000-s-prc-act says the canonical stays stable across every supersession, and the role fact 20260818-110100-s-prc-rol says a role binds to the name, so an identity fix owes no successor roles. The chain grouping's doc comment in `internal/model/chains.go` says it groups entries transitively linked by supersedes edges.

A model-level test with the same shape reproduces both effects. A single-target supersede keeps one chain and the role active. The grouping finds each entry's root through its first supersedes target only, so the second predecessor stays a chain of its own with no head. `ActorChains` sorts headless chains first and `ChainForCanonical` returns the first chain holding the canonical, so the role resolves to the headless one. Pre-flight's `parentActorChain` also considers only the first target. Procedure chains use the same grouping.
