SDD gives its users no way to feed back from their sessions, so the framework learns from real use only through raw material that its developers or early adopters collect, analyse and anonymize by hand. The feedback covers bugs, and also agent or sdd behaviour that falls short of what users expect. Many improvements to prompts and procedure instructions came from it. Improving SDD depends on that feedback, because its developers cannot anticipate every scenario real use brings.

**Actual.** Christopher has analysed many such cases. The material was coding agent output and other details pasted into a dialogue here, access to the agent's session log, or in the hardest cases sdd's own session logs. The actor merge defect 20261026-144315-s-tac-5y1 was found in another project's engine capture. The supersede content loss 20261029-115221-s-prc-u8v is behaviour short of expectation rather than a bug, and reached this graph when Christopher passed an agent's output from another project to a new session here. Some reports arrive as GitHub issues on this repository, such as brace literals breaking pre-flight in 20260906-231136-s-tac-ynt. A missing detail costs a round trip. The report of a generated summary carrying a literal `</think>` tag, 20261005-172136-s-tac-c8r, was first recorded with provider, model, thinking setting and sdd version unknown and requested from the reporter, and was replaced once the reporter's records arrived. sdd serves nothing for reporting: no move, no guidance on what to collect, no anonymization, no channel.

**Expected.** Stated by Christopher in this dialogue. Reporting is easy for any user. sdd tells the agent that a bug or unexpected behaviour can be reported this way, so the agent can offer it when one occurs. When a session behaves unexpectedly or fails, sdd guides the agent to gather what is needed to understand the issue and to anonymize it. The user confirms the report before it is sent, or answers the questions needed to understand the issue. The reason is scale. SDD is to reach more users, per the adoption focus 20260628-130020-d-stg-ous, and non-technical roles, per 20260422-122523-d-stg-x0l, and their feedback is what improves served instructions, procedures and the engine. Those users cannot be expected to copy agent output or hand over session logs. Anonymization protects the reporting project's material, and reports land in this public repository, where 20260505-110748-d-prc-zb6 allows no identifying content from other projects.

**Deviation.** SDD learns only from users able and willing to assemble raw material, and the users it aims to reach next are the least able to.

**Unsettled, from the dialogue.** Christopher's candidate is a procedure bundled with sdd that dispatches the gathering to a subagent, so the reporting session does not spend its context on it. The delegate path it would use is committed and not yet delivered: a task-class procedure forked into a non-interactive child session 20260827-232430-d-cpt-voa, and the harness-side dispatch artifacts 20260829-122157-d-cpt-zpe. Because the child is non-interactive, the user's confirmation and answers would stay with the procedure in the reporting session. Left open is how and where a report travels: channel, destination and format. Gathering would draw on the session log, which records no engine responses or errors per 20260707-170902-s-cpt-ev3. Reports would also feed the anonymized specimen corpus the eval directive 20261005-160413-d-cpt-3sl adopts.
