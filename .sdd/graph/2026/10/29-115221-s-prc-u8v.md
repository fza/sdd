A superseding entry drafted through capture loses content of the entry it replaces: the agent writes the successor from the dialogue rather than from the predecessor's body, so unchanged passages come back shortened, reworded, or missing.

**Expected.** The successor is the complete current picture: a reader of the head alone misses nothing and never needs the retired entry. Unchanged content carries over as written, only what the dialogue changed is changed, and the body states what changed and why. The capture procedure (20260703-094500-d-prc-cap) commits the outcome — a replacement carries the full current understanding on its own — and the plan kind's authoring fact (20260818-100000-s-prc-spc) has a successor plan cover the retired plan's remaining ground and name what changed. Carry-over as written is Christopher's expectation from this dialogue: a reworded passage lets an unnoticed change in, which loses information or adds content nobody decided.

**Actual.** Christopher has observed the loss in most supersedes reviewed. In the latest instance, a plan superseded in another project, the draft played back for confirmation had dropped worked examples, cut an enumerated scope carve-out down to its heading, and reworded unchanged acceptance criteria. Asked to compare old and new paragraph by paragraph, the same agent found and restored these passages at once — the comparison is within reach, the process never asks for it.

**Where it falls short — candidates, unsettled.**

- *Guidance states the outcome, not the method.* The procedure's one clause says what a replacement must achieve, not how: start from the predecessor body (or bodies, for a multi-target supersede), carry unchanged passages as written, edit only what the dialogue changed, state the changes. Neither the procedure nor a kind fact says what a good supersede looks like.
- *Brevity pulls the other way.* The entry-craft criterion (20260810-121912-d-cpt-7zr) asks for minimal text and repairs duplication by pointing instead of restating. Carried-over text looks like duplication but is not: once the predecessor retires, the successor owns that content. Nothing states this distinction.
- *No check compares before confirmation.* The writing guide judges the draft in isolation by design (20260810-141555-d-cpt-20r) and knows a supersede target only as an anchor for mentions. The only comparison is pre-flight's supersede check, which runs after the user confirmed the playback and flags only dropped essential concerns, so rewording and lost specifics pass. Two homes for a pre-playback comparison are open: the writing guide given the predecessor bodies, at the cost of the isolation its stranding test depends on, or the graph-fit guide that 20260810-141555-d-cpt-20r already scopes with supersede targets in detail, which is not built.
