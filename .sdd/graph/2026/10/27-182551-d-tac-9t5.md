Every place that tells a writer how to title an entry states one rule: commands, identifiers, file and package names, and any other name whose own spelling starts with a lowercase letter, go in backtick code spans in their own casing, and a title is 3 to 10 plain words, about 30 to 80 characters, as guidance that nothing enforces. This answers the gap that agents writing a title get none of the guidance the model gets (20261027-181119-s-tac-fgt), and widens the code-span plan (20261027-132914-d-tac-dcy).

## Behaviour

1. The title prompt (`internal/llmops/summary_templates/title.tmpl`) states the widened rule and the new length, with `sdd` and `macOS` as examples of lowercase names, in place of "at most 32 characters".
2. The capture procedure (`internal/baseprocedures/entries/20260703-094500-d-prc-cap.md`) states the rule and the length where an agent writes a corrected title: the verifySummary instructions and the `correctedTitle` field description.
3. The `--title` help on `sdd new` and `sdd summarize` states the code-span rule. The `sdd new` help no longer says the title is stored verbatim, because a first letter that is not a backtick is still capitalized.
4. No title length is checked. A title of any length is stored as written; the brief listing keeps cutting at 110 characters.

## Why

A name like `sdd`, `macOS` or `gollm` is a different word once capitalized, wherever it stands in the title, and the code span also tells a renderer that the span is a name to print as written. Capitalized proper nouns survive capitalization unchanged, so they need no span. Agents write titles in capture and through `--title`, so the rule must be stated there, at the moment of writing. A range of 3 to 10 words gives a title room to name its subject exactly where 3 to 5 words forced vague names.

## Rejected alternatives

- Every proper noun in a code span. People, products and languages would render in monospace as if code, and the model would have to judge what counts as a proper noun, so titles get less consistent.
- The rule in the sdd skill. The skill is a pointer to the engine that names features and when to use them; read at session start, it is far from the moment a title is written.
- A paragraph in the type-system overview fact. A further copy, read while drafting rather than when writing the title.
- A hard length limit that refuses longer titles, or a lint warning. A new refusal path or lint rule to maintain, and a generated title over the limit would cost another model call or a failed write.

## Accepted costs

The rule is stated in four places (the prompt, the capture procedure, two flag helps), and only tests keep their wording from drifting apart. Titles of up to 80 characters are longer than the short handles the short-titles gap (20261002-084539-s-cpt-9fp) asks for on dense reading surfaces. Backticks show as literal characters in plain-text listings.

Out of scope: stored titles keep their casing and length; regenerating them is separate work.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rendered title prompt states the code-span rule including names that start lowercase, and the 3 to 10 words, about 30 to 80 characters guidance, and no longer says at most 32 characters.
- [ ] The capture procedure's verifySummary instructions and `correctedTitle` description state the code-span rule and the length guidance.
- [ ] The `--title` help of `sdd new` and `sdd summarize` states the code-span rule, and the `sdd new` help no longer says verbatim.
- [ ] A title longer than 80 characters passed with `--title` is stored in full, with no cut or refusal for its length.
